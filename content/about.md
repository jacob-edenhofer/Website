I am a DPhil candidate in Politics at the University of Oxford. I aim to become a comparative political economist whose main applied research interest is climate politics. [Federica Genovese](https://federica-genovese.com/) and [David Rueda](https://www.nuffield.ox.ac.uk/people/profiles/david-rueda/) supervise my DPhil. My research examines how democracies can sustain decarbonisation when public support fluctuates and the costs of adjustment precede many of its benefits. I am particularly interested in how expectations of future electoral support affect the credibility of policy commitments, how that credibility influences investment by firms, and how their responses alter subsequent costs and political opposition. Related work examines whether compensation can sustain support when governments have limited information about losses, and when climate institutions and bargaining with producers encourage adjustment or protect established interests. I also study populism, redistribution and expert authority.

I try to resist the temptation to retrofit research questions to familiar methods. My aim is to use the methods best suited to the questions I find interesting, including methods I still need to learn. The title of my Substack, [*Often wrong, but sometimes useful*](https://jacobedenhofer.substack.com/), expresses a hope that errors can contribute to progress when others can examine their causes. [*Theory and Credibility*](https://academic.oup.com/princeton-scholarship-online/book/42453) encourages me to make explicit the assumptions connecting theoretical arguments to empirical evidence. [*Research Design in the Social Sciences*](https://book.declaredesign.org/) prompts me to consider whether a proposed design can answer the question that motivates it and under which circumstances it might fail. [Izzo, Dewan and Wolton’s work on cumulative knowledge](https://www.federicaizzo.com/pdf/ssrn-3239047.pdf) reminds me that accumulating findings requires attention to whether separate studies estimate comparable quantities. I hope to apply these lessons by making my reasoning open to criticism and recording informative failures in the [file drawer](/file-drawer/). These are demanding aims, and I expect to fall short of them often.
