I am a DPhil candidate in Politics at the University of Oxford, supervised by [Federica Genovese](https://federica-genovese.com/) and [David Rueda](https://www.nuffield.ox.ac.uk/people/profiles/david-rueda/). My aspiration is to become a comparative political economist whose principal policy domain of interest is climate policy in rich democracies. I examine how electoral incentives and organised interest groups interact when democratic governments address long-term policymaking problems, which involve imposing costs today for potential benefits in the future.

I also study how limited informational capacity constrains governments’ ability to target taxes and compensation, and when conflict between firms generates support for investment in fiscal capacity. Other projects examine how inflation affects demand for redistribution, how rising energy prices and local economic decline affect support for the populist right, and why electoral majorities delegate policy to technocrats when they fear losing power. As a young scholar, I am still developing my research profile and working out how best to balance the research in which I have a comparative advantage with the work most conducive to the accumulation of knowledge. I therefore expect my research interests to evolve.

This website also contains my [public writing and engagement](/writing/), [teaching materials](/teaching/), and details of my [professional service](/service/). In the [File drawer](/file-drawer/), I discuss my approach to research and share unsuccessful ideas and projects in the hope that others can learn from them.
