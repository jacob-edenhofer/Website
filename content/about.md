I am a DPhil candidate in Politics at the University of Oxford, supervised by [Federica Genovese](https://federica-genovese.com/) and [David Rueda](https://www.nuffield.ox.ac.uk/people/profiles/david-rueda/). I study how information and distributional conflict affect the capacity of democratic governments to respond to economic change. Climate politics is my principal applied interest. I am particularly interested in how electoral incentives interact with policies whose costs and benefits emerge unevenly across groups and over time. Related projects examine how citizens respond to economic losses, how governments acquire the capacity to target fiscal interventions, and how institutions allocate influence between voters, organised interests, and experts. As a young scholar, I am still developing a research profile within comparative political economy and working out where my comparative advantage might permit a useful contribution to the accumulation of knowledge.

In working on these projects, I am often torn between the ideals of modern social science and the incentives of academic publishing. Those ideals centre on a reflective equilibrium between theory and evidence, through which arguments guide inquiry and findings prompt revisions to our assumptions, explanations, and questions ([Ashworth et al., 2021](https://academic.oup.com/princeton-scholarship-online/book/42453)). This requires careful research design and attention to whether evidence from different circumstances supports the accumulation of knowledge ([Blair et al., 2023](https://book.declaredesign.org/); [Izzo et al., 2025](https://www.federicaizzo.com/pdf/ssrn-3239047.pdf); [Slough and Tyson, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/ajps.12742), [2024](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781009375856); [Findley et al., 2026](https://externalvalidity.com/book/)). Progress requires a willingness to be usefully wrong, although being right is welcome too—a sentiment reflected in my Substack, [*Often wrong, but sometimes useful*](https://jacobedenhofer.substack.com/). I try to resist retrofitting questions to convenient methods or promising results, knowing how difficult these ideals can be to achieve.

This website contains my [research](/research/), [writing for wider audiences](/writing/), [teaching materials](/teaching/), and [service](/service/), together with my [CV](/Jacob_Edenhofer_CV.pdf) and contact details. Its [file drawer](/file-drawer/) presents theoretical ideas and empirical projects that did not work out. I hope that sharing these attempts can help others learn from their limitations and avoid repeating unproductive approaches. I will sometimes manage these tensions more successfully than at other times. The website is therefore both a statement of purpose and a commitment device through which I hope to hold myself accountable to these aims.
